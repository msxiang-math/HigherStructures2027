Abstracts

Rea Dalipi Title: Teichmüller spaces with singularities and Virasoro coadjoint orbits Abstract: I will talk about Teichmüller spaces of hyperbolic metrics on a disk with ideal boundary and a marked point carrying a conical singularity of order $\beta$. For all $\beta>0$, we show that \(\operatorname{Teich}^{\beta}(\overline{\mathbb D})\cong S^1\backslash\operatorname{Diff}(S^1)\), and we construct natural closed 2-forms $\omega_\beta$ induced by the Atiyah–Bott construction. For non-integer \(\beta\), the form $\omega_\beta$ is symplectic, and one can identify \(\operatorname{Teich}^{\beta}(\overline{\mathbb D})\) with the corresponding elliptic Virasoro coadjoint orbit. For positive integers \(\beta=n\), $\omega_\beta$ is presymplectic, and it descends to the Kirillov-Kostant-Souriau symplectic form on the exceptional Virasoro orbit \(\mathrm{PSU}^{(n)}(1,1)\backslash\operatorname{Diff}(S^1)\).

Alberto Cattaneo Title: Reification of Gravity Abstract: The Batalin–Vilkovisky (BV) pushforward—defined as partial integration in the BV framework—induces a chain map between two BV theories that becomes a quasi-isomorphism under specific conditions. In such instances, it establishes an equivalence between the theories, allowing expectation values to be computed interchangeably. If the map preserves classical locality (i.e., up to corrections in $\hbar$), it still can alter the number of local degrees of freedom (roughly speaking, depending on whether one integrates out more physical or more ghost fields). We define a reification as a quasiisomorphic BV pushforward that (1) preserves classical locality, (2) originates from a topological theory (i.e., one lacking local degrees of freedom and invariant under diffeomorphisms), and (3) yields a theory with local degrees of freedom. The objective of this presentation is to demonstrate that four-dimensional Euclidean gravity emerges as a reification from a BF theory with a cosmological term based on a quadratic Lie algebra extending $\mathfrak{so}(4)$.
